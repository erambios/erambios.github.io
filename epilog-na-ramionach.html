Epilogue on Shoulders

Erambios Philosophical Essays

This is not transportation; it is a transfusion. At the precise moment when the oak seal of the coffin seems to buckle under the weight of all human history, an invisible blood pours from the wood and metal, mingled with the salt of unwept tears. In this mortal leakage bathe the last untouched memories: the fervor of first romantic raptures, the scent of a home from years past, the whisper of a long-departed mother. And beneath this gravity of grief lie only the shoulders—a life without a soul flows away like sewage, carrying its condensed experience into a new, unborn body, into a new era.

And here he is, the Pallbearer. With a face as immobile as a tombstone, he receives this torrent into himself. He becomes a temporary reservoir for that which is simultaneously inhuman and divine.

In his muscles, strained beneath the weight of wood and dust, suppressed unspoken feelings dwell—feelings of whose presence he is not even conscious. He feels a raw cold, yet does not know it is the cold of solitude left behind by those who remain. He feels an unsettling warmth, yet fails to recognize in it the echo of the last breath he himself is carrying. He thinks of bills, of weight in kilograms, of a steady pace. He does not know that beneath his feet a sacred ground yields: he is the last one upholding upon his shoulders the entire complexity of the human soul before it dissolves into nothingness.

And the final moment arrives. The ash-wood coffin slowly descends—upon ropes, with symbolic grace—into the dark abyss of eternal rest. The carrying ends. The physical load the Pallbearer bore vanishes. Yet instead of relief, a profound absorption takes place.

When the body, stripped of the weight of a pierced life, settles into the earth, the Pallbearer’s soul absorbs the eternal dilemmas of earthly pilgrimage. He is left with what is fleeting yet inalienable: the echo of guilt, the murmur of wisdom, the sense of injustice that managed to cling to his own unconscious essence. He steps away, but he is no longer the same individual. The pallbearer has departed; the passenger has returned, burdened for a moment with what is greatest and heaviest in every human life.

He remains alone with what has transpired, infused into the simplicity of his life, into the prose of days gone by. This baggage—this monumental, collective weight—remains within him forever. And worse still, with every subsequent burial, with every next coffin carried toward the abyss, this invisible burden only grows. And though unaware of his role, he will continue to carry it through the city and the streets, day after day, until his own final day.
