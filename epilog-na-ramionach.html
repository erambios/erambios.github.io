Epilogue on Shoulders

Standing Upon Historical & Philosophical Continuity

This is not transport; this is a transfusion. In the very moment when the oak casing of the coffin seems to strain under the weight of all human history, an invisible blood pours from the wood and metal, mingled with the salt of unwept tears. In this mortal leakage bathe the last, untouched memories: the fervor of first romantic awakenings, the scent of a home from years long past, the whisper of a long-departed mother. And beneath this burden, within this gravity of grief, lie nothing but shoulders—life stripped of its soul drifts away like a drain, carrying its condensed experience into a new, unborn body, into a new era.

And here he stands, the Bearer. With a face as motionless as a gravestone, he absorbs this torrent into himself. He becomes a temporary reservoir of that which is simultaneously inhuman and divine.

In his muscles, tense under the weight of timber and dust, unuttered emotions are stifled—feelings whose presence he is not even conscious of. He feels a raw chill, yet does not know it is the coldness of solitude belonging to those left behind. He feels an unsettling warmth, yet fails to recognize in it the echo of the final breath he himself is transferring. His mind is on bills, on weight measured in kilograms, on keeping an even stride. He remains unaware that beneath his feet, holy ground yields: he is the last one holding upon his shoulders the full complexity of the human soul before it expands into nothingness.

And the final moment arrives. The ash-wood vessel descends slowly—upon ropes, with symbolic grace—into the dark abyss of eternal rest. The carrying ends. The physical weight the Bearer bore vanishes. Yet instead of relief, a profound absorption ensues.

As the body, now free from the burden of a pierced life, settles into the earth, the Bearer's soul absorbs the eternal dilemmas of the earthly journey. What remains with him is that which is fleeting yet unalienable: the echo of guilt, the murmur of wisdom, the sense of injustice that managed to adhere to his own unmindful essence. He steps down, but he is no longer the same individual. The bearer has departed; the passenger has returned, burdened for a moment by that which is greatest and heaviest in the life of every human being.

He is left alone with that which has passed, poured into the simplicity of his life, into the prose of bygone days. This baggage—this monumental, collective weight—remains within him forever. And worse still, with every subsequent burial, with every coffin borne toward the abyss, this invisible weight only increases. And though oblivious to his role, he will continue to carry it through the city and the streets, day after day, until his own final day arrives.
