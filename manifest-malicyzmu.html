MANIFESTO OF ONTOLOGICAL MALICISM

By Tomasz Wiśniewski The Structure of Defective Being and Cognitive Malice

I. The Rejection of Privatio Boni

Evil is not a mere absence of good, a shadow, or an accidental flaw in the world's harmony (as the Augustinian tradition held). Evil within the structure of reality is a substantial, active foundation. Being is not neutral—it possesses an inherent mechanism of cognitive and structural "malice" (Ontological Malicism), which manifests most acutely when an individual approaches the truth about themselves and the universe.

II. The Architect's Design and the Axiom of Resemblance

The world bears the marks of a sadistic or defective design. Human beings were not created in the image and likeness of a merciful God; rather, their darkest impulses—cruelty, malice, the drive for dominance—are faithful reflections and resonances of the nature of Reality itself.

So-called "free will" in this systemic context does not serve the creation of good, but acts as a trap: a mechanism to burden creation with guilt for the flaws embedded in the Architect's own blueprint.

III. Intellect as a Resonator of Trauma

The defectiveness of the design reveals itself in the fracturing of human consciousness. Intellect was not granted to humanity to achieve absolute happiness, but serves as a resonator of trauma.

The higher an individual's intellectual capacity, the deeper their ability to perceive absurdity, the resistance of matter, and their own suffering. Consciousness becomes a lens focusing the malice of Being—allowing the victim to fully comprehend the hard, unyielding nature of the matrix in which they have been embedded.

IV. Clash with Being and the Craftsman's Response

When a human attempts to impose form upon reality, the resistance encountered (in labor, matter, body, or mind) is not coincidental. It is structural resistance. Reality actively opposes cognition and craftsmanship.

In moments of pure destruction or the mindless surge of chaos, human beings touch the very fabric of the Creator. Yet, the response to a world so constructed cannot be lamentation, but the Craftsman’s Rigor—the unyielding, precise application of the chisel at the point of contact, devoid of illusions regarding grace or the world's intent.
